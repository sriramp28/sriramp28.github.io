Computational fluid dynamics across natural and engineered systems

Sriram Kalathoor

How do structures, interfaces, and instabilities organize transport?

I combine high-fidelity simulation, mathematical diagnostics, and measurement-aware analysis to study reacting and compressible flows, propulsion, stratified oceans, double-diffusive mixing, and the structures that connect local fluid physics to engineering and environmental behavior.

Research program

One computational practice across two fluid domains.

Aerospace and geophysical flows differ in scale and purpose, but they pose many of the same scientific questions. I study how turbulence, coherent structure, finite geometry, reaction, stratification, and sparse observation determine what a system transports and what our models or measurements can reliably reveal.

01

Aerospace flows

Reacting turbulence, propulsion, acoustics, compressibility, thermal loading, and design-relevant transport.

02

Geophysical flows

Ocean mixing, stratification, double diffusion, polar heat pathways, and environmental transport.

03

Computation and analysis

Direct and large-eddy simulation, reduced models, numerical methods, coherent structures, and transport budgets.

04

Models and measurements

Verification, uncertainty, sparse sensing, environmental observations, and simulation-guided experiments.

Shared transport physics

From reacting shear layers to stratified ocean interfaces.

Across both domains, detailed calculations reveal where transport is organized and which structures a lower-cost model or sparse measurement must preserve. The applications differ, but the scientific habit is the same. Resolve the process, identify the mechanism, and test what remains when the model or observation changes.

Common questions, distinct applications

A broad mechanics of transport.

My work moves between engineered and natural flows without treating either as an extension of the other. Combustors, shear layers, thermohaline interfaces, and environmental plumes each provide a different setting in which to study multiscale transport, numerical fidelity, and the information carried by measurements.
