How do fluid interfaces trap, release, and reorganize heat and matter?

I combine high-fidelity simulation, observational data, and transport analysis to study stratified oceans, double-diffusive mixing, polar heat pathways, turbulent reacting flows, and the multiscale structures that connect local physics to environmental and engineering consequences.

Research program

Transport across natural and engineered systems.

My work asks when an interface insulates, leaks, overturns, or redirects transported material. Instability, coherent structure, finite geometry, sparse observation, and nonlinear exchange connect ocean thermohaline layers and environmental plumes with combustors, propulsion systems, acoustics, and engineered flows.

01

Earth-system interfaces

Ocean mixing, polar heat reservoirs, stratification, finite-depth exchange, and observable transport pathways.

02

Reacting and compressible flows

Combustion, acoustics, wave-vortex interaction, heat release, entropy transport, and turbulent mixing.

03

Simulation and design

CFD, finite-element and multiphysics modeling, propulsion, thermal-fluid systems, and design trade studies.

04

Data and computation

Direct simulation, environmental observations, numerical methods, HPC, and uncertainty-aware measurement reduction.

Current Earth-system work

From resolved plume forests to observed polar interfaces.

Five connected studies examine how inherited interface geometry selects salt-finger exchange, which parts of a route survive mean shear, what sparse hydrography can recover, and how finite polar interfaces separate cold upper water from subsurface heat reservoirs.
